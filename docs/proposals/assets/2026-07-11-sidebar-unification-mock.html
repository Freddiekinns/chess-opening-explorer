Design review · evidence engine · rev 2

The sidebar, unified

Rev 2 concedes the core critique: "most played" and the book's "continuations" are the same thing to a user. This version has one list of next moves, one Elo lens governing the whole column, and three panels with unmistakable jobs. Position shown: Slav 3.e3 Nf6, 1400–1800 selected.

One column, three roles

Lens

Elo selector

"Show this opening at my level."

Moved out of the stats panel to the top of the column — it filters everything beneath it: the win rates and the book's move stats. Persists site-wide as "my level".

Evidence

Win rates

"What happens from this position?"

Outcomes, volume, notable games, and the level check. No move list any more — numbers about this position only.

Navigation

Opening book

"Where am I, and what's next?"

The single home of next moves: named lines link onward, popular unnamed moves appear as data rows, all scored at the selected level. Overview (meaning) sits between the two, unchanged.

One list of next moves

The book's Continuations absorbs the explorer data instead of competing with it. The tree is the backbone — it always renders, even if Lichess is unreachable. Explorer data enriches it progressively: rows re-rank by what's actually played at the selected level, each gains a win/draw/win split, and popular moves that theory never named join the list as plain data rows.

1

The Elo selector becomes the column's lens

Labelled "Elo", ordered lowest to highest, sitting above everything it filters — the win rates and the book's move stats change together, so choosing a band never feels like a dead switch. Persists as "my level" site-wide.

Was: unlabelled pills inside the stats widget, affecting only part of the page.
2

Win rates is pure evidence now

Titled like its sibling panels, and it no longer carries a move list — that job moved to the book. What remains answers one question: how does this position score.

3

One source line answers "what am I looking at"

"Lichess games, 1400–1800 · live"; with no band selected it reads "Master games · updated Jul 2025".

4

The legend decodes the bar explicitly

Colour swatches tie each label to its segment and the labels get verbs: "White wins 48%". Sentence-case fixes throughout ("Total games", "Average Elo"). This legend teaches the encoding every mini bar below reuses.

5

Notable games collapses to three

Three visible, then the book's own "Show more" affordance.

6

The bridge card becomes one honest sentence

"These are everyone's results — analyse your own games in this opening." It closes the evidence panel's thought instead of interrupting the column.

Was: a standalone dismissable card ("see how you actually play the Slav").
7

The book absorbs the move stats — one list, not two

Continuations becomes "Next moves": named lines stay links (hover ring, orange move, chevron); popular moves theory never named appear as inert data rows — like Nc3, the actual club main line here. Every row shows the win/draw/win split at the selected level plus the games count. Alternatives gets the same treatment from the parent position's data. The tree is the backbone: with no explorer data the list still renders, just without bars.

Was: "most played" in one panel and "continuations" in another — the same subject twice with different clothes.
