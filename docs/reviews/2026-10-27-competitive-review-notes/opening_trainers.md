# Dedicated chess opening trainers and repertoire builders (as of October 2026)

Method note: WebFetch/curl were blocked, so everything below comes from
WebSearch result summaries (snippets), not full-page reads. Where a source is a
vendor writing about competitors it is flagged **[vendor-biased]**. Most "2026
comparison" articles found (chessatlas.net, chessflare.com, trychessmate.com,
checkmatex.app, chessiverse.com, raindropchess.com, darksquares.net) are written
by companies that sell an opening or chess-training product, so treat their
rankings and competitor prices with caution.

## Q1. What each product does, how training works, and what is free

### Takeaway

The field splits into (a) **course libraries with drills** (Chessable, Chessly,
ChessMood, Chessmate, Chessreps), where the line comes from an author, and (b)
**build-your-own repertoire trainers** (Chessbook, ChessAtlas, ChessFlare, Chess
Tempo, Listudy, Chessdriller, Chessboard Magic, Chess Prep Pro, Chess Opener,
Openings Mastermind, Chess Position Trainer), where the user's PGN, study or
games are the source. Almost all use spaced repetition. Free tiers are usually
capped by move or line count (200 to 400 moves, 200 variations, 20 lines, 5
lines per opening), with Pro at roughly US$5 to $13 a month.

### Inferences

- Chessable is moving towards build-your-own (Repertoire feature, trainable
  subvariations, Priority Lines), converging on Chessbook's model, while
  Chessbook leads on data-driven coverage (gaps, stats at your level, model
  games).
- The long tail of near-identical free trainers (many apparently launched
  2025–2026, often with SEO-heavy "vs X" pages) suggests SRS + PGN + game import
  is commoditised; differentiation is in data (what people at your level play)
  and content.

## Q2. Which mechanics are now table stakes?

### Takeaway

Spaced repetition over a move tree, PGN/Lichess-study import, Lichess +
Chess.com game import with deviation detection, and a free tier are
near-universal among 2025–2026 entrants. Gap detection and transposition
handling are common but not universal.

### Cited Findings

- SRS in essentially every product: Chessable, Chessbook, ChessAtlas (FSRS),
  ChessFlare (SM-2 per competitor), Chess Tempo, Listudy (Leitner),
  Chessdriller, Chess Prep Pro ("Smart Train"), Chessmate, Chessreps, Openings
  Mastermind — sources in Q1, e.g.
  [Listudy](https://listudy.org/en/blog/spaced-repetition-for-chess),
  [ChessAtlas pricing](https://chessatlas.net/pricing),
  [Chess Prep Pro](https://chesspreppro.com/)
- Game import with deviation flagging: Chessbook, ChessAtlas (free plan),
  ChessFlare sync, Chess Tempo (own games), Chessable Puzzle Connect (mistakes
  to puzzles) — [ChessAtlas pricing](https://chessatlas.net/pricing);
  [Chess Tempo](https://chesstempo.com/opening-training/);
  [Chess.com news](https://www.chess.com/news/view/announcing-chessable-3)
- Gap finding: Chessbook "biggest gap", Chess Prep Pro "Blind Spot Finder"
  (premium), Chess Tempo suggestions from real opponent lines —
  [Chessbook blog](https://chessbook.com/blog);
  [Chess Prep Pro](https://chesspreppro.com/)
- PGN import/export: Chess Prep Pro, Chess Opener, Listudy, Openings Mastermind,
  ChessAtlas ("paste a PGN") — [Chess Prep Pro](https://chesspreppro.com/);
  [App Store Chess Opener](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/chess-opener-pro/id1489282483)
- Transposition handling via FEN matching is called out explicitly by NeuralPGN
  and "Chess Opening Tool" —
  [search summary listing NeuralPGN](https://github.com/conqueran/lichess-opening-trainer);
  [PyPI chess-opening-tool](https://pypi.org/project/chess-opening-tool/0.1.2/)

### Inferences

- A user arriving from a Chessbook/ChessAtlas background will expect "connect
  your account → see where you left your line" as baseline.

### Gaps

- No systematic audit of which tools handle transpositions correctly; marketing
  rarely mentions it.

## Q3. Which mechanics are rare or novel?

### Takeaway

The less common, more defensible ideas are data-driven: opponent replies
weighted by real frequency at your rating, coverage/gap targets expressed as "1
in N games", model games matched to your repertoire, frequency-first learning
order, and a Maia-style human bot. Gamification (leaderboards, time trials, XP)
is used by mobile-first apps (Chessreps, Chessly). Trap filters are essentially
absent from dedicated trainers.

### Cited Findings

- Opponent move sampled by Lichess frequency: openingtrainer.com —
  [openingtrainer.com](https://openingtrainer.com/); stats split by rating band:
  Chessbook — [Chessbook blog](https://chessbook.com/blog); frequency-first
  learning priority and bulk-pruning rare moves: Chess Tempo —
  [App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/chess-tempo-chess-tactics/id1441878094);
  "Priority Lines": Chessable —
  [Chess.com news](https://www.chess.com/news/view/announcing-chessable-3)
- Coverage target "1 in 300 games" (Chessbook) —
  [64squares review](https://64squares.substack.com/p/chessbook-review)
- Model games tied to repertoire: Chessbook (45,000 games); Chessly courses
  include model games — [Chessbook blog](https://chessbook.com/blog);
  [old.chessly.com](https://old.chessly.com/courses/a7d23f32-20fb-4cf9-9b56-7e63329f55e7)
- Human-like bots for continuing past book: Chessly "Levi" (Maia), Chessboard
  Magic database/engine Practice Opponents, slippeee (Maia) —
  [ChessPivot](https://www.chesspivot.com/en/reviews/chessly);
  [Chessboard Magic wiki](https://wiki.chessboardmagic.com/Practice_Opponents)
- Leaderboards and 60-second Time Trials: Chessreps —
  [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6744000612); XP gamification: Chessly
  — [ChessPivot](https://www.chesspivot.com/en/reviews/chessly)
- Solitaire (guess the move through real games): Chessboard Magic v1.3 —
  [wiki Releases](https://wiki.chessboardmagic.com/Releases)
- Traps: no dedicated trainer found with a traps filter; an open-source trap
  scorer multiplies opponent move probability (Lichess API, configurable rating
  range) by win rate; a blog analysis found 1,146 of 1,193 opening × rating-band
  rows have zero trap hit rate —
  [chess-trap-scorer](https://github.com/davidADSP/chess-trap-scorer);
  [The Opening Club](https://theopeningclub.com/blog/best-aggressive-chess-openings)
  (single-blog analysis)
- Path/structured learning: Chessable's course chapters, ChessMood step-by-step
  repertoires by rating band (800–2000), Chessboard Magic "Playbooks" —
  [chessmood.com](https://chessmood.com);
  [wiki Releases](https://wiki.chessboardmagic.com/Releases)

### Inferences (implications for openingbook.xyz)

openingbook.xyz already holds the ingredients most trainers charge for:
rating-band explorer data (via its `/api/explorer` proxy), 12k opening pages,
videos, Lichess studies and game analysis. Adoptable mechanics, roughly in order
of fit:

1. **Rating-band-weighted practice opponent**: upgrade the fixed-line practice
   mode so black/white replies are sampled from explorer frequencies for the
   user's chosen band (openingtrainer.com, Chessbook). Note the explorer's 25
   req/min authenticated limit means this must lean on the existing cached
   proxy, not per-move live calls.
2. **"Where you left book" from game analysis**: the site already analyses
   users' games; flag the first move where they left the page's main line (or
   the most popular move at their band) and link to that position's page
   (ChessAtlas/Chessbook deviation detection, free-tier table stakes).
3. **Coverage/gap view without accounts**: "you meet this position in 1 in N
   games at your rating; the page has no line for it" (Chessbook biggest gap),
   computable from explorer stats.
4. **Model games**: link master/high-band games that reached the page's position
   (Chessbook, Chessly).
5. **Frequency-first ordering** of lines in practice (Chess Tempo, Chessable
   Priority Lines).
6. **Lightweight local SRS** (localStorage, no account), as tabia does, to avoid
   the free-tier caps competitors use; FSRS is the current marketing buzzword.
7. **Trap filter by rating band** is an unoccupied niche, but data suggests
   traps rarely fire, so present it honestly (and per the repo rule, never
   fabricate stats).
8. Leaderboards/time trials are proven in mobile apps but fit a reference site
   less well.

### Gaps

- No usage data on whether frequency-weighted opponents improve retention vs
  fixed lines.

## Q4. Onboarding patterns

### Takeaway

Onboarding is mostly "pick a ready-made repertoire or course" (free in
Chessbook, a library to fork in ChessAtlas, curated courses in
Chessmate/Chessreps/Chessly) or "connect your Lichess/Chess.com account". No
style quizzes were found in sources.

### Cited Findings

- Chessbook: free pre-made repertoires to start from —
  [Pre-made repertoires](https://chessbook.com/repertoires/)
- ChessAtlas: build from scratch, paste PGN, or fork from a curated library —
  [MWM listing](https://mwm.ai/apps/chess-openings-chessatlas/6770867404)
- Chessreps: 25+ curated repertoires; Chessmate: 123 courses —
  [App Store Chessreps](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6744000612);
  [trychessmate.com](https://www.trychessmate.com/)
- ChessMood: repertoire split by rating (simplified set for 800–2000) —
  [chessmood.com](https://chessmood.com)
- Chessly: 7-day trial without card —
  [ChessPivot](https://www.chesspivot.com/en/reviews/chessly)
- Chess Prep Pro: no account needed —
  [chesspreppro.com](https://chesspreppro.com/)

### Inferences

- "No account needed" is a selling point for free tools; openingbook.xyz can
  match it.

### Gaps

- No source described a quiz- or style-based onboarding ("are you aggressive?");
  I could not confirm whether any tool uses one. Chessbook's onboarding flow
  specifically was not documented.

## Q5. Evidence of scale and popularity

### Takeaway

Chessable is the only product with clear scale (500K+ Play downloads, 4.5 from
~17K reviews, "2 million+ players" claimed). Chessreps shows modest traction
(10K+ Play downloads, 4.8). Most others have little public evidence.

### Cited Findings

- Chessable: 500K+ downloads, 4.5 from 17K reviews, claims 2M+ players; AppBrain
  estimates ~960K total downloads —
  [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.chessable.chessable&hl=en_US);
  [AppBrain](https://www.appbrain.com/app/chessable/com.chessable.chessable)
- Chessreps: 10K+ Play downloads, ~2.55K reviews at 4.8; 111 iOS ratings at 4.8;
  ~6.1K downloads in 30 days per one tracker —
  [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.chessreps.app&hl=en_US);
  [App Store AU](https://apps.apple.com/au/app/chessreps-master-openings/id6744000612);
  [AppBrain](https://www.appbrain.com/app/chessreps-master-openings/com.chessreps.app)
- ChessAtlas: Product Hunt 0 upvotes, #350 of the day; outside the top 30 in US
  Education (MWM) —
  [hunted.space](https://www.hunted.space/dashboard/chessatlas);
  [MWM](https://mwm.ai/apps/chess-openings-chessatlas/6770867404)
- Chess Opener PRO: 80 iOS ratings (4.4); ~92–110 Android reviews —
  [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/chess-opener-pro/id1489282483);
  [AppBrain](https://www.appbrain.com/app/chess-opener-pro/com.chessopenerapp.pro)
- Listudy: ~373 GitHub stars —
  [AlternativeTo](https://alternativeto.net/software/listudy/about/)
- Chessly: the parent channel is GothamChess (Levy Rozman), which is very large;
  no Chessly user count found —
  [Wikipedia: Levy Rozman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levy_Rozman)

### Inferences

- Outside Chessable (and probably Chessly, given Levy's audience), the
  dedicated-trainer market appears small and fragmented; a free reference site
  with high organic traffic could plausibly reach more users with a light
  trainer than most of these apps have.

### Gaps

- No download/user figures found for Chessbook, ChessFlare, Chessly, ChessMood,
  Chessmate, or Chess Prep Pro.
